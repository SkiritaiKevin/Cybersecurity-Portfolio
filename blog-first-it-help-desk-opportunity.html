My First IT Opportunity at La Salle

I am excited to be starting my first real IT opportunity in Tier 1 IT Help Desk Support at La Salle University. After spending so much time building projects, taking classes, improving my resume, and looking for the chance to gain professional experience, it feels good to finally have somewhere to apply what I have been learning. I know this role will be different from working through a lab where I control the environment and can pause whenever I need to. The problems will belong to real people who need to get back to class, work, or whatever they were trying to accomplish before something stopped working. That responsibility makes me a little nervous, but it is also exactly why I am excited. I want to learn what it feels like to support a real organization, contribute to a team, and become somebody people can rely on when technology gets in their way.

I expect Tier 1 support to teach me that good IT work is about much more than knowing a list of technical fixes. There will be tickets involving accounts, passwords, software, hardware, connectivity, permissions, printers, and problems I have not seen before. Some issues will probably be solved in a few minutes, while others will require careful notes, troubleshooting, research, or escalation to another person. I want to become better at asking the right questions, separating symptoms from the actual cause, documenting what I tried, and knowing when I have enough information to move a ticket forward. I also expect to learn how much prioritization matters. A small inconvenience for one user and an outage affecting an entire classroom cannot be treated the same way. Building that judgment, while still giving every person patience and respect, is one of the most valuable things I think a help desk can teach.

The social side of the role may be just as important for me as the technical side. Reaching out, speaking confidently, and working through an unfamiliar problem with another person have not always come naturally to me. It is easy to feel comfortable troubleshooting alone because there is no pressure to explain every step or admit immediately when I do not know something. At the help desk, I will have to get past that hesitation. I want to become comfortable introducing myself, listening without jumping to conclusions, asking follow-up questions, and explaining a solution without hiding behind technical language. I also want to learn that saying “I do not know yet, but I will find out” is not failure; it is often the beginning of honest support. Every ticket, phone call, and in-person conversation will be a chance to build confidence, and I think becoming a stronger communicator will make me better in every technical role that follows.

My long-term goal is still to grow into a cybersecurity professional, and I see this opportunity as the beginning of that path rather than something separate from it. Security depends on understanding how people actually use accounts, endpoints, networks, applications, and institutional systems every day. Working in support should give me a clearer view of identity and access problems, device configuration, common user mistakes, suspicious behavior, documentation, escalation, and the operational decisions that keep an environment running. I want to absorb as much as I can, continue developing my labs and research outside of work, and gradually connect what I see at the help desk to the security concepts I have been studying. My next step is not to rush past the role in search of a title that sounds more advanced. It is to become dependable at the opportunity I have been given, build a strong technical and professional foundation, and use that experience to move toward cybersecurity with a much better understanding of the systems and people I hope to protect.
