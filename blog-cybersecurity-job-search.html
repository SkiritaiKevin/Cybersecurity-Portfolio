Looking for My Place in Cybersecurity

I've been looking for my first real opportunity in cybersecurity, and honestly, the market is rough right now. A lot of entry-level listings already have hundreds of applicants, and plenty still want experience that is hard to get before somebody gives you that first chance. It can get discouraging. I still open the job boards, look through what is out there, and try again.

My setup is pretty simple. I have this portfolio for showing what I've worked on, GitHub for the actual files and evidence, and my resume for the quick version. I also keep a lot of notes. Whenever I build something, I try to write down what I changed, what broke, how I fixed it, and how I knew the fix actually worked. That has helped a lot when I go back to write a project page or practice explaining a lab out loud. Without the notes, I know I would forget half of the useful details.

I've also made a project roadmap for myself because it is really easy to keep starting new things and never fully finish any of them. The roadmap gives me something to return to when I am not sure what to work on next. Finishing my Active Directory lab was a big part of that. I got to work with identity, permissions, Group Policy, Windows events, PowerShell logging, troubleshooting, and hardening, and then I put the evidence on GitHub instead of just saying I did it. My networking and AI research projects cover different ground, which is important to me. I want the projects to show that I can learn a system and stick with it, not just follow a setup guide once.

The applications have become their own routine. I look for roles that actually make sense for where I am, adjust what I submit when a position calls for it, and track where I've applied so it doesn't all blur together. I pay attention to the skills that keep showing up too. Sometimes that gives me a clear idea of what I should review or build next. A lot of applications go quiet. Some turn into rejections. Every once in a while, one turns into an actual conversation, and that is enough to make me keep sending them.

I've been reaching out to people more as well. That part does not always come naturally to me, but submitting an application into a portal and disappearing probably isn't enough in this market. I connect with people doing security work, follow companies I care about, ask questions when I have a real one, and share my work when it is relevant. I am not messaging strangers expecting them to find me a job. Mostly, I want to learn how people got where they are and get better at talking about what I can already do.

Some days it feels like I am doing a lot without moving very far. I can finish a project and still not get an interview. I can spend time on an application and never hear anything back. That is probably the hardest part: there is not always an immediate sign that the work is paying off. But I am better at explaining Active Directory than I was a few months ago. My portfolio is better. My notes are better. I understand more, and I have more proof of it.

So that is where I am right now. I am building, taking notes, following the roadmap, applying, reaching out, and trying not to let the quiet days decide how I feel about the whole process. I do not know which application or conversation will finally be the one that opens a door. I just know I plan to be ready when it happens, and I am going to keep pushing until it does.
